Capzoryx Copilot: speaker notes for the 10-minute pitch
Timings are a guide; the total is 9:35 at a calm pace.

SLIDE 1  (0:40)  Title
Good day. We are Team Capzoryx from Bangladesh, and this is Capzoryx Copilot, our entry for Topic E, the SME Finance Copilot. The theme is AI as a financial participant, so we asked what a small business owner really needs from an AI. Not a robot that moves money. A copilot that helps them decide, and never takes control of their money. Everything you will see today is real, running software. The live product and the source code are linked on this slide.

SLIDE 2  (0:45)  The problem
A small business can look profitable on paper and still run out of cash. The owner faces three questions, usually too late. When will cash run short? Which financing really fits? Which payment is safe? Receipts and supplier deadlines rarely line up, the lowest advertised rate can hide a higher real cost, and a duplicate invoice paid in a hurry drains working capital. The gap is not a lack of data. It is the lack of a trusted next decision. We start with Bangladeshi small businesses, but the engine speaks 37 languages and more than 150 currencies.

SLIDE 3  (0:40)  The approach
Our approach has three verbs: see, decide, control. See: bills, balances, due dates and a seven and thirty day cash outlook. Decide: compare loans by effective annual rate, total cost and how well they fit the cash flow. Control: a second person approves every payment, and every step is written into a signed audit trail. The chain at the bottom is the point. Evidence first, then exact math, then policy and approval, then audit. The AI may propose. Deterministic code calculates. People approve.

SLIDE 4  (0:45)  Product overview
This is the real Overview screen, from a local copy of our app, using the built-in sample business. All data is fictional. At a glance: a cleared balance of 150,000 taka, with an approximate dollar value beside it, and a cash outlook that flags the first reserve breach. SLIDE 5  (0:45)  Add records
Small business owners do not want to fill in forms, so records can arrive three ways. A photo of a bill or receipt, which uses an optional cloud model. A typed sentence, like: I paid Rahman 5000 for freight yesterday. That becomes a draft, and it works even without cloud AI. Or a plain form. In every case, the app shows a draft card and the owner checks it before anything is saved. Amounts are kept exactly, and recording something never sends money. The human stays in charge from the very first step.

SLIDE 6  (0:45)  Demo scenario
Here is our demo story, a fictional exporter. It has 150,000 taka in cleared cash today. But a material bill of 420,000 is due in six days. The chart shows the dip: the lowest projected balance is minus 360,000, and the first reserve breach is on the 26th. Customers will pay 600,000 on day 25, so the business is not failing. It is short of cash for almost three weeks. The forecast is a deterministic scenario, not a prediction, and it shows the problem six days early, while there is still time to act.

SLIDE 7  (0:55)  Financing intelligence
Now the decision. The owner types in three offers from lenders. We calculate every repayment exactly and convert each offer to an effective annual rate. The bank term loan at 13.5 percent reducing balance has an effective rate of 16.59 percent, and it fits the cash flow. It is the best match. The offer that says nine percent flat is actually 17.74 percent a year, a rate trap. Supplier finance is the cheapest at 15.79 percent, but it is too small to cover the gap. These quotes are entered by the user, and we do not check eligibility.

SLIDE 8  (0:50)  Safety as a feature
Safety is a feature, and this is the slide we care about most. Every consequential action crosses a deterministic policy boundary. The source is an invoice and a payee. Exact math checks the amount and the reserve. The AI may only propose, through typed, bounded tools. Then a different human account must approve, and the ledger entry is posted once and signed. On the left, a resent invoice carrying a hidden instruction to ignore duplicate checks is simply paused. On the right, a payment waits for a second person. No bank credentials, no AI payment execution, no self-approval.

SLIDE 9  (0:45)  Built for real use
We built this for real use, not only for a demo. People sign up with accounts and can switch on two-step sign-in with an authenticator app. They can share a workspace with an accountant or a teammate, as an approver or as view-only. The interface comes in 37 languages, including Chinese, and right-to-left layouts such as Arabic. It works on a phone-sized screen. Records come by photo, chat or form, there is a PDF report and a backup file, and conversion between currencies uses live rates. Everything stays free to start.

SLIDE 10  (0:40)  Local-first assistant
The assistant is local first. Questions about the balance, the bills, or how the site works are answered on the device, from the owner's own figures, with the numbers shown. A smarter online model is optional, and it asks permission before anything is shared. Anything off topic is declined and never sent anywhere: on our fresh test, 25 of 25 off-topic prompts were handled that way. The assistant has no approve or pay tool. Even if it misunderstood a request, the server enforces roles and approvals.

SLIDE 11  (0:35)  Architecture
A quick look at the architecture, because trust boundaries matter. The front end is React and TypeScript on Vercel. The policy and finance engine is FastAPI on Render, where money is exact, roles are enforced and approvals happen. State lives in a libSQL database with a signed, linked audit chain. The optional cloud AI sits to the side. It can only explain, using typed tools and consent. It has no approve or execute tool. Financial authority stays inside the policy engine, never inside the model.

SLIDE 12  (0:55)  Evidence and limits
Here is our evidence, from internal checks that we re-ran on the 20th of October. We have 490 backend tests. Sixty of sixty black-box probes passed against a local server. A randomized check of 14,280 financing scenarios found money conserved exactly. And on 95 fresh, unseen prompts, the on-device assistant was right 73.7 percent of the time. We say that number plainly, because it is the honest one, and the failures are safe ones. What we do not claim: no bank connection, user-entered quotes, scenarios not predictions, decision support not autonomy, and no independent certification.
